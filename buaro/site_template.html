What prevents heat deaths: measures

Two independent lines of evidence side by side. Cooling measured from space: more street trees where people live lower the surface temperature (step 3); only part of that reaches the air, since across 392 European urban areas trees cool the daytime air about 12 times less than the surface (Du et al. 2024). Difference between areas: greener areas lose fewer people per degree of heat (step 2); bringing each area up to the best-covered area of Attica stays inside what Attica already shows, and includes everything that comes with greener neighbourhoods, so it is an upper bound.

    Impact: what this software changes

    BUARO turns a framework from a book into a working, self-updating instrument. Every claim below rests on the results above and on official European data.

    1. It counts a hidden toll, every month, without anyone running it

    Heat deaths are rarely recorded as such on death certificates. BUARO finds them in the official weekly deaths of Eurostat, area by area, and updates the count on its own every month. In Attica it estimates about one thousand heat-related deaths in an average summer, and over two thousand in the summer of 2024, in line with the independent estimate of ISGlobal published in Nature Medicine.

    2. It shows where to act first

    The same degree of heat is far more dangerous in some parts of Attica than in others: . Civil protection and health services can send heat warnings, cooling centres and home visits where each degree costs the most lives, instead of spreading them evenly.

    3. It separates what works from what only looks green

    From space, BUARO measures that street trees cool the city surface, the same way in every summer it has never seen, while open lawns do not. It then shows, with a published European ratio, that cooler air alone explains only a small part of why greener neighbourhoods lose fewer people. This tells a municipality what a tree-planting programme can be expected to deliver, and that shade where people walk and wait, cooler homes and care for the most exposed matter as much.

    4. It speaks the language of the new EU Climate Resilience Law

    The EU climate resilience framework of October 2026 sets reference levels of 1.5°C, 2°C and 3°C of global warming for planning. BUARO answers directly in those terms: how many more heat deaths each level would bring to each area of Attica, and how many each measure would prevent, using the CMIP6 projections of the Copernicus Interactive Climate Atlas and the warming levels of the IPCC.

    5. It can be trusted, because it can be checked

    Only official and peer-reviewed sources are used. Every model is tested on summers it has never seen, uncertainty is always shown, and a claim is reported as a finding only when it survives those tests; when it does not, the page says so. All data and code are open, and every number on this page can be downloaded and verified.

    6. It is ready for every European city

    Only the area codes and a few coordinates in BUARO are specific to Athens. Weekly deaths by area come from Eurostat, land use and street trees from the Copernicus Urban Atlas, which maps 790 Functional Urban Areas of more than 50,000 inhabitants, surface temperature from Landsat and climate projections from Copernicus. The same software can give every one of those cities the same instrument, at no cost, as a ready building block for Local Digital Twins and for the climate risk assessments that the EU framework calls for.
